The art of the marginal note

Long before software, readers argued with books in their margins. A marginal note is a small act of attention: it marks the exact place where a thought happened, and it only makes sense next to the words that caused it.

Pierre de Fermat famously wrote that he had a truly marvelous proof which this margin was too narrow to contain. The note outlived its author by centuries, and the margin became part of the story.

Digital annotation has to solve a problem paper never had: the text moves. Paragraphs are edited, pages are re-rendered, and the words under a highlight may change. A good engine follows the text when it can, and admits defeat when it cannot.

Try highlighting a phrase, then press Mutate content. Then try highlighting a phrase that appears twice, like the margin, and edit around it.
