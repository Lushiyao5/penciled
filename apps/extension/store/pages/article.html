The Slow Reader

Reading with a pencil in hand

An essay on marginalia · 6 min read

For centuries, readers have written in their books. Medieval monks drew pointing hands in the margins, and students underlined the lines they meant to remember. The habit survived because it works: marking a passage forces you to decide what matters.

Reading on a screen quietly took that habit away. Articles scroll past, tabs close, and the sentence that changed your mind last week is impossible to find again. Bookmarks keep the page, but not the part of it you cared about.

A highlight is a small act of attention. It says: this, here, is the point. A note goes one step further and records why. Together they turn reading into a conversation with the text, and with your future self.

The best tools for this get out of the way. They appear when you select a sentence, and disappear when you are done. They never change the page itself, and they remember your marks when you come back.
